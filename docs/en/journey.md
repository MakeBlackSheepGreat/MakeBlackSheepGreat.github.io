# Journey

A timeline of what I have done, from the first circuit board in middle school to the direction I am working on now. Not a highlight reel — several stages went badly, and I keep them here because each later decision was more or less pushed into place by the one before it.

## Middle school · First contact with hardware

I joined the Arduino club at school and got my hands on circuits, code and sensors — line-following robots and similar projects — and entered a few provincial and municipal competitions, winning a first prize at provincial level and a second prize at municipal level in Guangdong. Back then I could not have explained what "embedded" meant. I only knew that a few lines of code plus a few wires made a toy car turn, and that felt remarkable.

## High school · Self-study and tinkering

Two things took most of my time. The first was self-study: the basics of Linux, Python, and HarmonyOS ArkTS development, which ended with the Huawei HarmonyOS application developer certification. The second was tinkering with Android rooting — flashing ROMs, bricking phones, recovering them, poking around system partitions. Looking back, none of it was systematic, but it built two habits: look things up myself first, and refuse to believe I have learned something until it actually runs.

## 2025.09 · Starting university

I entered Southwest University of Science and Technology, admitted to Pharmaceutical Engineering in the School of Life Science and Agriculture. That did not match my interests, so from the first day I kept drifting towards computing and electronics: sitting in on lectures, joining technical clubs, looking for projects.

## 2025.10 · Joining the "One Student One Chip" study group

Invited by a senior in the CS club, I joined the One Student One Chip project and taught myself Verilog and RISC-V processor design, starting from basic digital logic and reaching the E5 stage of RTL simulation, reporting progress at a group meeting every two weeks.

This was the hardest learning I have done: no lab manual, no final exam, just a goal and a codebase far beyond my level. What I actually gained was not Verilog — it was knowing the boundaries of my own self-study ability, and which things I can grind through alone versus which need a person to ask.

## 2026.03 · CS lab, and catching up in parallel

I started working in a cross-disciplinary technical environment, and sat in on the automation department's circuit analysis course up to chapter seven. I spent that period taking pharmaceutical courses during the day and reading circuits and code at night; coursework and interest were completely split, which was uncomfortable.

That was also when I realised interest alone gets you nowhere — every application asks what you have actually built. So I started approaching teachers and looking for projects.

## 2026.04 · Joining the medical imaging lab

I joined my current lab (Dong 6b 501) and started working on medical imaging projects. It was the first time I worked with a teacher on a real project, and my role changed from "following along" to "I write this": data processing, model training, result visualisation, and eventually the whole demo system.

I learned how a segmentation annotation for an ultrasound image is actually produced, and how easily a classifier's score can be inflated by the way the data is split.

## 2026.05 · Three things at once

- Second prize at the university-level biomedical engineering innovation design competition for a breast ultrasound CAD system — my first end-to-end run through topic selection, experiments, documentation and defence
- Selected for the "Military Information Technology" innovation class
- A provincial undergraduate innovation training project I am part of was approved

## 2026.06 · Provincial second prize and JLPT-adjacent milestone

- Provincial second prize, 6th Sichuan Provincial Biomedical Engineering Innovation Design Competition
- Passed the College Japanese Test Band 4 (CJT4)

## 2026.07 · National third prize

- National third prize, 11th National College Student Biomedical Engineering Innovation Design Competition
- Finished a complete MNIST engineering project: seven models (BP, CNN, ConvNeXt, LeNet-5, ResNet, ViT, MobileNetV2) compared under one dataset and evaluation protocol, wrapped in a Flask API, a web frontend and an Electron desktop app. Code is open source.

## 2026.07 – 08 · Summer: betting on engineering

I stayed on campus that summer and spent it on three things:

1. **Putting agents into my real research workflow** — a multi-agent collaboration system, workflow tooling for literature and experiments, and a pipeline that ingested 30,000 papers into a local knowledge base (11 hours, 99.96% success, over 18,000 evidence links)
2. **Filling in the engineering side** — deploying model serving and web services on a self-hosted server, from private networking and reverse proxies to process supervision, learning from mistakes the whole way
3. **Touching domestic AI chips** — entering several CANN operator contest problems: convolution, activation and quantised GEMM kernels

## 2026.09 · Transferred successfully

I moved into Electronic Information Engineering (Honors) at the School of Information and Control Engineering, and entered the CUMCM mathematical modelling contest the same month. I had been preparing for the transfer since my first semester — grades, sitting in on lectures, the lab, competitions — all of it so that I would have something to say when the moment came.

There are quite a few courses to catch up on, but the direction finally lines up.

## What is next

- Catch up on the electronics core curriculum, especially the maths and signals foundation
- Keep going on medical imaging: reproducible experiment records for the ongoing project, and the methodological details written up properly
- Prepare for competitions such as the electronic design contest and the Loongson Cup
- Pass JLPT N2
- Go deeper rather than wider: I have plenty of things that run, and too few that would survive a serious follow-up question
